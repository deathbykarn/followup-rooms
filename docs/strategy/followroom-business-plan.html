01

Executive summary.

The whole story, briefly

Relationship-driven salespeople win or lose on what they remember between meetings. FollowRoom is the memory layer that makes sure they never forget — built, validated, and days from its first paying pilot.

A property agent manages dozens of live relationships across WhatsApp, voice notes, and viewings. The deals that close are the ones where the client felt remembered — the wife's preference for Marine Parade because her parents are nearby; the hesitation about repayments after bonus season. That texture lives in the agent's head and three chat apps, and the moment it slips, so does the trust. A $3,000-a-month assistant still can't replay last month's viewing on demand.

FollowRoom turns the messages an agent forwards and the meetings they record into a living, client-specific memory — every detail linked to its source — and a polished, co-branded room the agent can share with the client. The operator owns the relationship. We own the memory. The product is silent by design: it never messages the client, and nothing reaches them without the operator's approval.

The product is already built — live backend, WhatsApp ingestion, transcription, and AI extraction with evidence receipts. Two qualified industry figures independently validated it: a 12-year veterans realtor designed the product on a call (“I give it to you”), and a Showsuite director designed the go-to-market (“sell it as the digital auntie, not AI”).

The economics are software-grade: ~93% gross margin per seat. The path is profitable without venture-scale outcomes — 250 paying seats (under 1% of Singapore's registered agents) reaches $20,000/month in profit. We are raising $350K to fund the pilot, the first growth cluster, and the road to profitability — not the road to fundraise again.

02

The problem.

What's broken

Deals aren't lost on the pitch. They're lost in the gaps between conversations — where memory fails and trust quietly erodes.

Relationship-driven salespeople — real estate agents, insurance advisers, private bankers, IFAs — carry their most valuable asset in their heads: the specific texture of every client relationship. They manage 50–200 active relationships at once, scattered across WhatsApp threads, voice notes, viewings, and ad-hoc meetings.

The tools meant to help don't fit how they work. CRMs demand manual data entry into structured fields and never capture the unstructured texture that actually matters. Note-taking is too slow to do live. A human assistant costs ~S$3,000/month and still can't recall a specific past conversation on command.

So the texture stays trapped in recordings and chat history that are impossible to navigate. As one veteran agent put it: “I still need to go through the minutes. I don't even know which minute, which second we had this topic about — it's not like YouTube.”

And there's no good artifact to give the client afterward — a long WhatsApp text is ignored, a PDF takes up space. The follow-up, the moment that builds trust, is a manual, unstructured, unshareable afterthought. That is the gap FollowRoom closes.

03

The solution & product.

What we built

Forward it. We remember the rest. FollowRoom converts the agent's existing workflow into structured memory and a shareable client room — without asking them to change how they sell.

01

Silent ingestion

The operator forwards a WhatsApp message or uploads a meeting recording. No new app to live in, no channel to change.

02

Memory, built

Speech is diarised and transcribed; key facts are extracted and filed under the right client, each one linked to its exact source.

03

Operator approves

The dashboard surfaces what was captured. The operator accepts, edits, or rejects. Sensitive inferences never reach the client.

04

The client room

A co-branded, timestamped, threaded link the client opens on any device — meeting notes, next steps, and documents, all approved by the operator.

Built, not slideware. Live FastAPI backend on Render; Next.js dashboard on Vercel; Supabase data layer with per-operator security; WhatsApp Cloud API ingestion; AssemblyAI transcription; Anthropic-powered extraction with evidence receipts. 130 backend tests, CI on every change. Money raised funds growth, not the core build.

08

Competition & moats.

Why it holds

The product is copyable. The moats are structural, slow to assemble, and uncomfortable for incumbents to imitate because they impose constraints incumbents won't accept.

i.

Memory compounds

Every message and meeting deepens the client record. At month thirty of a relationship, switching cost is thirty months of texture — not a database export. Competitors enter every relationship at month zero.

ii.

Trust as architecture

Evidence receipts, operator-approval gates, no sensitive inferences in client surfaces, no third-party scripts. These read as features but are coded invariants — the convenient shortcut is the one that breaches trust.

iii.

Channel-native onboarding

Forward a message — that's the activation step. Most CRMs die here. The silent WhatsApp ingestion behind it takes quarters and a regulatory posture (Business Verification, PDPA) to replicate end-to-end.

Adjacent players — Salesforce (Keller Williams' CRM partner), HubSpot, agency-internal builds, call-analytics tools like Gong — either demand structured data entry, prescribe behaviour, or aren't channel-native. FollowRoom competes by staying sharper, smaller, and trust-first. The explicit non-goal: never try to become Salesforce.

Risks & mitigations.

Named honestly

The most dangerous risks are the unnamed ones. These are designed-against; none, in our judgement, is existential. A deeper internal risk register is maintained separately.

R-01Adoption, not just endorsement
An “I love it” is not daily use. Mitigation: the beta measures weekly active usage and willingness-to-pay rigorously before any scale.
R-02Median-user activation
Our lead beta user is a sophisticated outlier. Mitigation: the pilot includes less tech-forward agents; onboarding must work without hand-holding.
R-03Meta / WhatsApp dependency
Ingestion sits on Meta's rails. Mitigation: channel-agnostic at the boundary; Telegram + email forwarding parity is a known, small lift.
R-04Unvalidated pricing
$99/$149 is a reasoned hypothesis, not proven. Mitigation: the beta is the price-discovery instrument.
R-05Single-founder bandwidth
Solo to month 6, with other commitments. Mitigation: first revenue funds the first hire; Phase 1 is operationally light by design.
R-06Data & trust liability
Handling third-party client conversations carries PDPA exposure. Mitigation: trust architecture + operator-responsibility framing + privacy by design.
