The verdict, up front

The sole prop is faster and cheaper to start. But it cannot take investment, exposes you personally, and can't be sold — so for FollowRoom, its only advantage is illusory.

You'd have to convert to a Pte Ltd before the first dollar of investment anyway. Converting mid-raise is more friction than starting right. Incorporate once, now.

01

The decisive reason.

If you read nothing else
Structural blocker

You literally cannot take the investment into a sole prop.

A sole proprietorship has no shares. A SAFE converts into equity — and there's nothing for it to convert into. The entire $350K SAFE @ $3M-cap raise in the investment package is structurally impossible as a sole prop. The choice isn't really “sole prop vs Pte Ltd” — it's “incorporate now, or incorporate later under deal pressure and re-paper everything.” Since you know you're raising, doing it once, now, is the clean path.

03

Two more, once you're profitable.

Tax & credibility

Tax efficiency

Sole-prop profits are taxed as your personal income — progressive, up to 24%. A Pte Ltd pays a flat 17% corporate rate, and Singapore's Start-Up Tax Exemption exempts 75% of the first S$100K of chargeable income (and 50% of the next S$100K) for the first three years. At your projected $20K/month profit, the corporate structure is materially more efficient and gives you salary-vs-dividend flexibility.

17% flat · vs · up to 24% personal

Credibility & onboarding

Investors effectively require it — but it goes further. Meta Business Verification, Stripe, banks, and agency / enterprise customers all onboard a Pte Ltd more smoothly and take it more seriously. A Pte Ltd UEN reads as “a real company,” which matters at every counterparty touchpoint from your first pilot invoice onward.

One clean entity for BV · banking · Stripe · the SAFE
04

The honest case for sole prop.

And why it loses here

The sole prop's advantages are real — just marginal against four structural blockers. Worth stating plainly so the decision is made with eyes open.

Genuine advantages

  • Cheaper: ~S$115 first year, ~S$30–90 to renew.
  • Simpler: no company secretary, no annual returns, lighter accounting.
  • Faster to set up and to wind down.
  • Fine if you're only testing and never raising.

Why it loses for FollowRoom

  • Pte Ltd is ~S$315 self-filed via BizFile — still ~1 day.
  • Ongoing overhead is modest: company secretary (~S$300–600/yr) + annual filings.
  • If you're a SG citizen or PR, you can be the resident director yourself — no nominee cost.
  • Its one edge (a few hundred dollars) is dwarfed by: no investment, personal liability on a PII product, no equity to grant, nothing to sell.
The only scenario where sole prop wins: you decide not to raise and run FollowRoom purely on cashflow as a solo operation. The investment package says otherwise — so Pte Ltd it is.
05

How to do it.

Practical path

Roughly one day and ~S$315 self-filed. Sequenced so the new entity feeds straight into Meta verification and the raise.

  1. Incorporate the Pte Ltd via BizFile — name application + incorporation. ~S$315 self-filed, or ~S$600–1,000 via an agent (Sleek / Osome / Lanturn) who bundles the company secretary.
  2. Confirm the resident-director requirement. At least one director must be ordinarily resident in Singapore. If you're a citizen or PR, that's you — no nominee director needed.
  3. Appoint a company secretary within 6 months and set minimum paid-up capital (S$1 is fine). ~S$300–600/yr if outsourced.
  4. Pull a fresh ACRA Business Profile from the new Pte Ltd — this is the document Meta Business Verification wants. ~S$5.50.
  5. Use the one clean entity everywhere: Meta BV, business bank account, Stripe, and the SAFE all reference the same UEN. Avoids the re-papering you'd hit if you started as a sole prop.
